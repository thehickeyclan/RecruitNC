# Super 32 2026 — North Carolina Girls Recap

## Overall Assessment

Super 32 is the best yardstick we have. Once a year the country's best high school wrestlers come to one tournament in Greensboro, and we get to see where North Carolina stands. Not only against every state in the country, but against ourselves: the North Carolina of a year ago, and the year before that.

By that measure, the top of North Carolina's girls' lineup has arrived. Four years ago North Carolina brought 32 girls to this tournament and came home with one medal. This year 42 girls wrestled, and two of them, Lauren Samuel and Ella Taylor, finished 3rd: the best finishes by any North Carolinian, boy or girl, all weekend. The medal count dipped, from three last year to two, but both of this year's medals are bronze, and they were won the hard way. Samuel and Taylor beat six wrestlers between them who also stood on the podium.

The weekend had its leaders. Samuel (Cleveland, 132), a freshman, went 7-1 and became a two-time Super 32 All-American; after losing her quarterfinal she beat the girls who finished 7th, 5th and 4th. Taylor (Cary, 200), unseeded, beat the #3 seed and lost only to the champion.

Not every number moved. Samuel and Taylor were North Carolina's only girls with winning records; North Carolina's girls went 26-82, and behind the medalists the best weekends were 2-2. Twenty-seven of those losses came against eventual medalists, and the gap between the top two and the next tier is the work in front of the state. The four-year comparison below shows where we've grown and where we haven't yet.

## How the Grades Work

These are performance grades for one weekend, not rankings of who is the better wrestler. A grade measures what a wrestler did in Greensboro: who she beat, how far she went and who beat her. A win over a national seed carries the most weight, because most NC girls were unseeded and every seed they beat was a result the bracket didn't expect. The rules are the same ones the boys' recap uses, word for word.

Each grade has one rule, printed beside it above, and a wrestler gets the highest grade whose rule she meets. An A+ takes a top-four finish on the podium; two North Carolina girls reached it this year.

Only contested bouts count toward a grade's wins over seeds; a forfeit or injury default counts in the record, never in the grade. Every grade above follows its rule; none was set by hand. Within each grade, wrestlers are not listed in rank order. Wrestlers below that line are named in Honorable Mention or in The Rest of North Carolina.

## A+

**Lauren Samuel (Cleveland, 132) — 7-1, 3rd place, All-American.** The best finish by a North Carolinian all weekend, and her second Super 32 medal after placing 7th a year ago. The #12 seed opened with three straight technical falls, the first over Alyssa Ornelas, Colorado's 4A champion, and the third over #5 Kailin Sebert of Texas, #4 nationally (MatScouts), in the round of 16. Her only loss was a 7-7 criteria decision to #4 Mila Mantanona in the quarterfinal. Then Samuel out-wrestled the bracket: a tech fall over Kit Alsaker, a 12-3 decision over #8 Victoria Carbonaro, a fall over #3 Rachel Garcia in the consolation semifinal, and a 10-0 tech fall over #6 Olivia Bezdicek, Idaho's state champion, in the 3rd-place match. After the quarterfinal she beat the wrestlers who finished 7th, 5th and 4th, and the girl who beat her finished 6th.

**Ella Taylor (Cary, 200) — 4-1, 3rd place, All-American.** Unseeded, and it didn't matter. Taylor beat #3 Kenzie Barrett, Florida's state champion, 9-4 in the round of 16, then pinned Blake Baker of Texas in the quarterfinal. She lost her semifinal to #2 Zeinab Mourad of Florida, who went on to win the tournament. She finished the job in the consolations: an 8-4 decision over Delina Gritz, New York's 4th-place finisher, then a 5-0 shutout of Autumn King, #14 nationally (SI), in the 3rd-place match. Her other three wins came over the wrestlers who finished 7th, 6th and 4th; her only loss was to the champion.

## A-

**Yaleen Khang (Maiden, 100) — 2-2.** Khang beat #8 Piper Phillips of Iowa, #11 nationally (SI), on criteria, 6-6, then lost her round of 16 to #9 Cidney Schaffer of Pennsylvania, who finished 6th. She teched Anela Hogg of Washington, #24 nationally (SI), 10-0 in the consolations before going out to Massachusetts state champion Olivia Polansky, one win from the blood round. A seeded win and a loss to a medalist: the strongest weekend of any North Carolina girl who didn't medal.

## The Rest of North Carolina

Twenty-six more North Carolina girls stepped on the mat in Greensboro, and the draw was unkind: Mia Pardo, the #7 seed at 95, went 0-2 and was eliminated by the wrestler who finished 7th, and Mackenzie Shaver lost to the eventual champion at 138. Super 32 doesn't hand out easy weekends, and every one of them took the hardest draw in high school wrestling on purpose.

## The Bigger Picture: Raising the Bar

Super 32 is one of the toughest tests in high school wrestling, and a chance to measure North Carolina against the best competition in the country. This year's girls' results give us reasons to be encouraged.

Lauren Samuel and Ella Taylor showed that North Carolina girls can beat the best wrestlers in the country: between them, six wins over girls who stood on the podium, and two bronze medals.

The results also show how much work remains. We produced two girls' All-Americans, and only two winning records out of 42 wrestlers. The top is here; the depth behind it is what has to grow.

That's the standard we're chasing.

At NC United, our mission isn't only to celebrate individual results. It's to raise wrestling across North Carolina and measure our progress against the best in the country. Super 32 gave us another benchmark. Now the challenge is to build on it.
