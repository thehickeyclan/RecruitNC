# Super 32 2026 — South Carolina Boys Recap

## Overall Assessment

Super 32 is the best yardstick there is. Once a year the country's best high school wrestlers come to one tournament in Greensboro, and every state gets to see where it stands: against the rest of the country, and against itself a year ago.

For South Carolina, the middle of the lineup took a real step. Nine SC boys left Greensboro with winning records, the most in the three years measured, and South Carolina won more matches than in either of the last two years with a smaller group. Three SC boys beat national seeds, led by Sy Strobel's 10-6 win over the reigning NHSCA sophomore national champion, and Rollins Dixon won five matches, including one over a wrestler ranked #11 in the country.

The next step is the medal rounds. No SC boy placed this year, and the deepest runs, by Dixon and Strobel, ended one round short of the blood round. The year comparison below shows where South Carolina has grown and where it hasn't yet.

## How the Grades Work

These are performance grades for one weekend, not rankings of who is the better wrestler. A grade measures what a wrestler did in Greensboro: who he beat, how far he went and who beat him. A win over a national seed carries the most weight, because most SC boys were unseeded and every seed they beat was a result the bracket didn't expect. The rules are the same ones used for every state.

Each grade has one rule, printed beside it above, and a wrestler gets the highest grade whose rule he meets. An A+ takes a top-four finish on the podium; no South Carolina boy reached it this year.

Only contested bouts count toward a grade's wins over seeds; a forfeit counts in the record, never in the grade. Every grade above follows its rule; none was set by hand. Within each grade, wrestlers are not listed in rank order. Wrestlers below that line are named in Honorable Mention or in The Rest of South Carolina.

## A-

**Rollins Dixon (St James, 165) — 5-2.** The 2026 SC 5A runner-up won five matches. He opened with a decision over Chaney Lewis Jr. of Pennsylvania, a Fargo 16U Greco placer, then lost 4-2 to #4 Thunder Page of Kansas, who finished 5th. In the consolations he beat Missouri's Reed Wilson, #11 nationally (Flo), 5-4, pinned Virginia 4A runner-up Rocco Zagorites, and beat #10 seed Manny Gusfa of New Jersey, an NHSCA placer, 11-4. His run ended against #16 Grayson Debevoise of Ohio. Both of his losses came to seeds.

**Sy Strobel (West-Oak, 190) — 3-2.** A two-time SC 3A champion, Strobel won his first three matches on the championship side, the third a 10-6 decision over #13 Dresden Beard of Kansas, the 2026 NHSCA sophomore national champion. He lost to #4 Mason Savidan of California, who finished 3rd, and then to Tennessee's Rock Shurette, an NHSCA placer.

**Maximiliano Johnson (James Island, 150) — 4-2.** Johnson won four matches, including a 7-0 shutout of NHSCA placer Micah Engelman of Pennsylvania and a 6-1 decision over #25 seed Deacon Morgan of Michigan, #7 nationally (Flo). His losses came to #3 Peyton Hornsby of Indiana, who finished 2nd, and Georgia 3A champion Cruz Hezel.

## B

**Jake Strickland (Hillcrest, 113) — 3-2.** A two-time SC 5A champion, Strickland won three straight in the consolations, technical falls over West Virginia's state runner-up and Georgia's 6A 3rd-place finisher, then a fall. Both of his losses came to seeds: #6 Connor Maddox of Indiana, who finished 6th, and #26 Lucas Layne of Florida.

**Taylon Winker (Catawba Ridge, 120) — 3-2.** Winker won three in a row in the consolations, including a technical fall over Illinois's 2A runner-up and a major decision over Florida's 2A runner-up. His losses came 10-8 to NHSCA placer Preston Bubash and to Ohio's Division I champion, #18 nationally (SI).

**Bryson Chappell (Chesnee, 126) — 3-2.** The 2026 SC 2A/1A champion won three straight in the consolations, including a 5-0 shutout of Ohio's 2025 Division II runner-up and an 8-5 decision over Michigan's Division 2 champion. He opened against #13 Tyler Verceles of Maryland.

**Ryan Osolin (132) — 3-2.** Osolin opened with a 9-3 decision over Virginia's 3A champion, then teched Virginia's 5A runner-up in the consolations. His losses came to #12 Cayden Rios of Texas, the NHSCA runner-up, and Pennsylvania placer Jake Benyo.

**Xavier Gonzalez (Clover, 144) — 3-2.** Gonzalez won three straight in the consolations, including decisions over Florida's 3A 3rd-place finisher and Indiana's 6th-place finisher. Both of his losses came to seeds: #18 Kavi Garvey of California, who finished 7th, and #19 Nicholas Marchese of Illinois.

**Tyrone Norris III (Clover, 157) — 3-2.** The 2026 SC 5A runner-up beat Florida's Howard Hill, the 2026 NHSCA freshman national champion, 10-2, and North Carolina's 6A runner-up 4-1. Both of his losses came to seeds, the second 4-3 to #21 Guardian Miller of Ohio.

## B-

**Montero Royal (Goose Creek, 120) — 2-2.** A three-time SC 5A champion, Royal opened against #1 Cameron Sontz of New Jersey, the eventual champion. He answered with wins over Florida's 1A 3rd-place finisher and South Carolina's 2A/1A champion.

**Zander Mueller (126) — 2-2.** Mueller beat Louisiana's Division 2 runner-up 13-6 and Florida's 2A 3rd-place finisher 10-7. He opened against #9 Tommy Gretz of Pennsylvania, who finished 8th.

**Noah Lynch (138) — 2-2.** Lynch pinned his opener and then Rhode Island's state champion, Logan Medeiros, in the consolations.

**Jacob Rasamee (James Island, 144) — 2-2.** Rasamee opened with a 13-6 decision over Georgia's 4A runner-up and later beat North Carolina's 7A 3rd-place finisher 7-4. He lost to #9 Caden Woodall of Arizona, the NHSCA national champion.

## The Rest of South Carolina

Thirty-eight more South Carolina boys stepped on the mat in Greensboro, and every one of them chose the hardest test in high school wrestling.

## The Bigger Picture

Super 32 is one of the toughest tests in high school wrestling, and a chance to measure South Carolina against the best competition in the country. This year's results give the state real reasons to be encouraged: nine winning records, more wins than in either of the last two years, three wins over national seeds, one of them over the reigning NHSCA sophomore national champion, and the NHSCA freshman national champion beaten in the consolations.

The next step is the podium. That's the standard every state is chasing, and Super 32 gives South Carolina a clear benchmark to build on next year.
